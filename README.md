# Can We Use Data To Predict People's Behaviour on Social Media
(Disclaimer - The dataset used in this project is fully synthetic. The results of this analysis should not be interpreted to reflect the real world.)
## Background & Overview

I set out to conduct research on the effects that social media has on us as a society and searched for a means to understand what about social media causes damage as well as the characteristics that leave us susceptible to being negatively influenced by it.
This dataset covers countries all over the would with people ranging in age from 13-65, and a range of professions.
Insights and recommendations are provided in the following areas:
- **Purchases based on user behaviour:** How the behaviour of a user can predict their likelihood to make purchases on social meda

## Executive Summary

One of the questions I investigated is whether we could predict whether or not a user was more likely to make, or not make, a purchase, and I found that there were a few factors that seemed to have an influence on users in this regard.
One of these factors was their primary purpose for their use of social media. People who primarily used social media for “News & Updates”, “Learning” and “Business/ Marketing had 1-2% higher average of people who did not make purchases on social media in comparison to people who used social media primarily for “Socialising”, “Networking” and “Entertainment”. I believe this to be because those in the former group are all using social media as a form to improve themselves in some regard and are therefore less likely to make unnecessary purchases on social media. Contrastingly, the latter group are primarily using social media for fun, which would make them more likely to want to purchase things that they see.

Another factor that has an effect on whether a user is more or less likely to make a purchase on social media is what their peak usage time is. Those who scroll after 12am, between 12pm-4pm and between 6pm-9am have approximately 3-5% more people on average who do not make purchases on social media in comparison to those who scroll between 6am-10am and between 9pm-12am. This is most likely due to people being at work and with their families between the hours 12pm-9pm. More purchases tend to be made at the start or end of days suggesting they are made when the normal day is either yet to start or is unwinding, so how much a user is on social media during these times can have an effect on how likely they are to make purchases

## Recommendations
Based on the insights that have been made, here are the following recommendations for those using social media:
-	**Avoid excessive scrolling before work or late at night** as this is when you are most susceptible to making purchases that may not be needed
-	**Consume educational content as well as other content** as this is likely to keep you more grounded and motivated and prevent you from making purchases on a whim that may not be necessary
